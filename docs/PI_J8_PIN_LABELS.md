# Raspberry Pi 3 Model B V1.2 J8 photo pins

`annotations/pi-j8-pin-labels.json` records 40 physical GPIO header contacts in `IMG_20261009_222248_1.jpg`, the highest resolution phone view for this board. Each pin has a pixel box, the existing `major-003` header parent, the physical J8 pin number, and the net name printed in the revision 1.2 reduced schematic. The companion [review overlay](../previews/photo-pins/pi-j8-222248-review.png) shows all 20 rows against the original photo.

The photo visibly reads **Raspberry Pi 3 Model B V1.2**, and the printed `J8` is beside the top of the two-row header. The inner column is the odd-numbered column; the outer column along the right board edge is even-numbered. Pin 1 is the upper inner contact, by the chamfered header outline. The [Raspberry Pi forum's connector explanation](https://forums.raspberrypi.com/viewtopic.php?t=216304) independently describes odd contacts on the side away from the PCB edge and the chamfered pin-1 corner. The collected revision 1.2 [reduced schematic](../data/board-sources/raspberry-pi-3b-v1.2/reduced-schematic.pdf) labels J8 pins 1 through 40 and their nets. The [mechanical drawing](../data/board-sources/raspberry-pi-3b-v1.2/mechanical.pdf) confirms the header position on this board outline.

The mapping includes 3V3 on pins 1 and 17, 5V on pins 2 and 4, ground on pins 6, 9, 14, 20, 25, 30, 34, and 39, and the documented GPIO and ID nets on the remaining contacts. These are **physical connector numbers**; for example physical pin 11 has source net `GPIO17`. The boxes select the visible metal within each contact opening, rather than an entire plastic cavity. Pin locations in lower rows have moderate visual confidence because their metal is less sharp. The pin ordering and schematic names have high confidence from the visible orientation cues and revision-matching source. No native PCB pad export or electrical test is present for this specimen, so the source net names are documentary links rather than measurements.

Only this one image is labeled. The other Pi photos include crop variants of the same physical board and need separate image-coordinate boxes if used for pin training or evaluation. Component coverage elsewhere on this board remains partial.
