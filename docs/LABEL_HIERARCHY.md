# Board, part and pin labels

The [label hierarchy](../data/photo-model/label-hierarchy.json) applies the same **board → part → pin** order to ESP32, ESP8266, Ultimainboard 2.1.4, NXP FRDM-MCXN236 and Raspberry Pi 3B V1.2. It joins reviewed board labels, existing component seeds, available linked design sources and the shared [photo pin annotation intake](../annotations/pin-annotations.json). Run `python scripts/build_label_hierarchy.py --check` to confirm it still matches those files.

Every one of the 80 images has a reviewed board family and box. Nineteen images currently have partial component seeds: 236 part boxes in total, including an [expanded visual review](EXPANDED_COMPONENT_SEEDS.md), a [validation-photo sprint](COMPONENT_SPRINT.md) and three ESP contact-row parents. Ten NXP parts have readable photo reference labels linked to visually reviewed Rev C schematic symbols and BOM records. Those ten source parts provide 352 distinct pin records with source pin numbers and nets.

Four J22 contacts on the photographed Ultimainboard 2.1.4 have reviewed pixel boxes and physical pin numbers 1–4. The revision marking, J22 reference and pin-1 triangle match the version-matched PCB pad order. A [verified source link](J22_SOURCE_LINK.md) joins those pins to `+5V`, `GND`, `TXD2`, and `RXD2`. The [J23/J1/J2 expansion](ULTIMAINBOARD_PIN_EXPANSION.md) adds 18 numbered, source-linked contacts with separately checked orientations. The generator applies its three parent review updates and validates source hashes, schematic pin indices and unique through-hole pad UUIDs. The 352 NXP source records under ten candidate photo parts remain unregistered. A separate [source catalog](SOURCE_PIN_COVERAGE.md) holds 151 ESP8266-reference and 1,292 Ultimainboard schematic pin records; 22 records are joined to reviewed photo pins.

The [ESP contact labels](ESP_HEADER_PIN_LABELS.md) add 15 ESP32 and 29 ESP8266 contact boxes under three reviewed parent rows. These 44 labels use the exact printed alias, such as `D15` or `G`, with `pin_identifier_kind: board_silkscreen_alias` and `pin_number: null`. Source nets and refdes are unknown. A printed `D15` is not asserted to mean physical pin 15. The generator checks photo hashes, row containment and separate silkscreen boxes. Raspberry Pi has no reviewed photo pin positions. All five families follow the same labeling rule; unknown pins are never used as negative training examples.

For example, the photographed NXP board contains a seeded MCU SWD header marked J12. The Rev C source lists J12 pin 1 on `JTAG_VREF`, pin 2 on `P0_0/SWDIO` and pin 4 on `P0_1/SWCLK`. The photo shows the header and the J12 marking. Assigning those numbers to individual photographed contacts still requires registration to the PCB layout or an independently checked pin-1 orientation.

A [J12 review proposal](NXP_PIN_REGISTRATION_SPRINT.md) now records ten visible contact boxes and candidate Rev C pad/net numbers. It remains outside the canonical photo pin labels because the photographed revision and pin-1 mark have not been confirmed.

The next review must follow this order for each photo, regardless of board manufacturer:

1. Confirm board family, model and revision where visible. Keep unknown revisions explicit.
2. Review each visible part box, class and reference marking. The current 236 boxes are partial seeds.
3. For each visible contact or lead, identify its physical pin number from an oriented layout or legible pin-1 evidence, record its photo box and identity evidence in `annotations/pin-annotations.json`, then attach a source net only when the source matches the photographed board. BGA balls and hidden pins have no photo label from a top-view image.

Each numbered photo pin entry needs a unique `id`, `image_file`, the matching `image_sha256`, exact `board_family`, reviewed parent `part_id` from the generated hierarchy, `pin_number` as a string, `photo_bbox_xyxy_pixels` within that image, `pin_identity_evidence` explaining orientation or marking, and `review_status: visually_reviewed_pin_location`. The generator checks the photo hash, board and part parent before accepting the pin. An entry can have a reviewed physical pin number without a source net; that status stays explicit. Printed-alias contacts use the separate ESP intake and identifier kind. If the pin cannot be numbered reliably, do not assign a numeric training label.

Board identification, source component identity and physical pin position have separate evidence requirements. Component and pin model training waits for reviewed, sufficiently complete photo labels and independent validation images.
